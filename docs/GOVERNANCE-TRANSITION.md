# Governance Transition — Moving Toward a Community DAO

## What would actually be governed

Not the software/hardware licenses (MIT + CERN-OHL-P are already decided
and don't need a DAO to hold them). A future DAO's real scope:
- Curated app-repo approval decisions (which partner/community apps get
  merged — see BOM.md's app-repo model)
- Roadmap prioritization for the "deferred/nice to have" tier
- Treasury stewardship (grant funds, donations, any future community
  funding)

## Why not day one

Two concrete reasons to wait, not just caution for its own sake:
- **Grant accountability.** Foundation and Commons grants are structured
  around milestones with a single accountable point of contact (see
  FUNDING-STRATEGY.md). A DAO trying to execute milestone-based grant
  deliverables by committee is a real friction point grant-makers
  recognize and are wary of — keep single/small-team ownership through
  grant execution.
- **Premature decentralization slows early decisions.** Before there's a
  working v1, most decisions are fast, technical, and don't benefit from
  distributed input — they benefit from one or two people building
  quickly. A DAO with no real usage yet is governance theater, not
  community ownership.

## The actual precedent worth following: Teia's own transition

Teia didn't start as a DAO — it started as a **Core Team Multisig**
handling day-to-day operations, and only moved into a **DAO beta phase**
once there was real scale: thousands of members, a token distribution
completed, and enough active community participation for governance
votes to mean something. The multisig didn't disappear immediately
either — it stayed legally/operationally responsible during the DAO beta
period specifically to avoid exposing a new, untested governance
structure to real exploit/mistake risk before it was proven.

This project should follow the same shape, not invent a new one:

1. **Now → post-launch**: single owner / small founding contributor group.
   Fast decisions, direct grant accountability, direct relationship with
   the audit contact and any funding bodies.
2. **Post-M11 (community readiness) + grant milestones complete**: move
   treasury and repo-merge authority into a **small multisig** among
   founding contributors — mirrors Teia's Core Team Multisig stage. This
   is the point where grant funds and any donations need transparent,
   accountable, but still fast-moving stewardship.
3. **Once there's a real, active multi-contributor community** — meaning
   independent contributors beyond the founding group actually submitting
   apps/fixes, not just users — consider a formal DAO structure (token or
   multisig-expansion-based) for roadmap and app-repo governance
   specifically. Keep the multisig in a supervisory role during this beta
   period, same as Teia did, rather than handing over full control
   immediately.

## Concrete triggers, not just a date

Don't set a calendar deadline for this — gate the transition on real
conditions being true:
- Treasury holds funds significant enough to need accountable, transparent
  multi-signer stewardship (not before)
- Multiple independent contributors are actively submitting to the
  curated app repo, not just using the device
- Community size large enough that governance participation is
  meaningful rather than symbolic

If none of these are true yet, it's not time — a DAO without real
participation is worse than honest single ownership, since it creates
governance overhead without governance substance.
